## 6. What an Exact Corner Certificate Proves

A certificate lists a rational side $S$ and, for each square, its corners
$c_0, c_1, c_2, c_3$ with rational coordinates.
Three facts are decided over $\mathbb{Q}$ with no tolerance:

1. **Each square is a unit square.** With $e_i = c_{i+1} - c_i$: $e_i \cdot e_i = 1$,
   $e_i \cdot e_{i+1} = 0$, and the corners close, $e_2 = -e_0$ and $e_3 = -e_1$. Two
   consecutive perpendicular unit edges and closure make the corners the vertices, in
   order, of a square of side one.
2. **Each square lies in the container.** Every corner coordinate is in $[0, S]$; the
   square is the convex hull of its corners and the container is convex.
3. **No two interiors meet.** For each pair, some direction among the four edge normals
   projects the two squares onto intervals that meet at most at an endpoint.
   The line perpendicular to it through a separating point has each interior strictly on
   its own side. By the separating-axis theorem for convex polygons such a direction
   exists for every pair with disjoint interiors, so a valid packing always passes.

The $n$ squares are then a packing of unit squares in a square of side $S$, which is the
definition of $s(n) \le S$. Nothing is scaled at decision time: the dilation of §7 is
applied before the test, and the object decided is the packing itself.

The hypotheses each checker assumes:

- **H1.** $s(n)$ is taken over closed unit squares with disjoint interiors, any position
  and rotation, in a closed square container, the case records’ scope.
  Contact is allowed: a gap or a clearance of exactly zero passes, and a negative one
  fails.
- **H2.** The four corners of a square are listed in cyclic order.
  Both checkers test it through the edge and closure conditions, so a misordered square
  fails, not passes.
- **H3.** Separating-axis soundness: an axis whose intervals meet at most at an endpoint
  gives a separating line.
  Completeness is needed only so that a valid packing is not refused.
- **H4.** Exact arithmetic: Python’s integers and `fractions.Fraction`.
- **H5.** The loader returns the numbers in the file, the declared $n$ equals the number
  of squares, and the ids are distinct.
  The promotion’s loader and the independent checker both test the count and the ids.
- **H6.** The side the case record carries is at least the certificate’s $S$. That is
  `catalogue_upper_bounds check`, which derives the receipt’s verified value from the
  certificate’s side line; the case records are not yet written.

## 7. The Promotion

`promote_rational`, driven with the settings of `upper_bound_packets` (36 rational
digits, side increase at most $10^{-9}$), turns each centre-and-angle witness into a
corner certificate:

- **Centres** are rounded to 36 significant digits.
  The parse’s centres carry at most 17, so they are unchanged.
- **Angles.** $t = \tan(\theta/2)$ is computed at 56 digits and rounded to 36
  significant digits, and the rotation is $((1 - t^2)/(1 + t^2), 2t/(1 + t^2))$, whose
  squares sum to one exactly.
  Each angle moves by about $10^{-36}$ radians.
  One square at $n = 83$ has the parse’s angle $3.4 \times 10^{-49}$ degrees; it becomes
  a tiny nonzero rational rotation, which is why that certificate’s common denominator
  has 417 digits.
- **Dilation.** The centres, and not the squares, are moved away from the container’s
  centre by the first factor of $1, 1 + 10^{-31}, 1 + 10^{-29}, \ldots, 1 + 10^{-3}$
  that passes the exact test of §6.
- **Side.** The squares are translated so that the least $x$ and least $y$ are zero, and
  $S$ is the largest remaining coordinate: the certificate’s own extent.
  All three certificates touch their container on the left, bottom and one far side.
- **Acceptance.** $S$ must be at most the witness’s side plus $10^{-9}$. That allowance
  is never approached.

## 8. Two Checkers and What They Share

**The promotion’s test**, `sqpack.verify.verify_packing` with exact rational signs, runs
inside the promotion on every candidate and again on the committed file through
`exact_verify`, both in `packing-witness verify` and in `catalogue_upper_bounds check
--replay`. It loads the file through `sqpack.yamlio.load_yaml`, which refuses duplicate
keys, validates it against the witness schema and checks the count and ids.
It decides unit squares by edge lengths and right angles at all four corners,
containment corner by corner, and every one of the $n(n - 1)/2$ pairs by the four edge
normals.

**`devtools.check_rational_witness_independent`** loads the file through
`sqpack.yamlio.safe_load`, which allows duplicate keys, and checks the envelope, the
representation, unit size, the coordinate convention, the count, distinct ids and four
two-string corners. It decides unit edges, consecutive orthogonality and closure, the
least containment clearance, and for every pair the best gap over the four edge normals,
which must be non-negative.

**What they share:** Python’s integers and `Fraction`, PyYAML through `sqpack.yamlio`,
the certificate’s bytes, and authorship in this repository.
No geometry or verification code is shared.
The two loaders differ on duplicate keys, and the regular expression that reads the side
for the receipt takes the first `side:` line; each certificate has exactly one `side:`
line and one `n:` line, checked here, so they read the same numbers.
Neither checker decides provenance, and neither needs to for the bound.
The promotion’s correctness is not part of the trust boundary: whatever it produces is
decided afterwards, so a promotion fault could cost tightness and never soundness.

**The negative controls** mutate the $n = 69$ certificate, and each is refused by both
checkers, which the test decides again rather than reading back:

| Control | Independent checker | `exact_verify` |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Side cut by $10^{-15}$ | Refused: container penetration $-1/10^{15}$ | Refused |
| Square 1 moved right by $10^{-6}$ | Refused: one overlapping pair, gap $-999999999/10^{15}$ | Refused |

Both are meaningful.
Because each certificate is tight, the first shows that containment is decided at the
smallest cut the printed precision expresses.
The second shows that the pair test sees an overlap, but at $10^{-6}$, seven to ten
orders of magnitude above the certificates’ least gaps; a control that pushes a touching
square into its neighbour by twice its least gap would test the pair decision at the
scale that matters (KB-9).

## 9. The Review’s Own Checker

`verify_cert.py` was written for this review from §6 and shares no code with either
checker above or with any YAML library.
It reads the certificate text line by line, puts every coordinate over one common
denominator (174, 417 and 173 digits), and makes every test in integer arithmetic: the
ids are exactly $1 \ldots n$, each square is a unit square by the edge and closure
tests, every corner lies in $[0, S]^2$, and every pair passes the separating-axis test
on the four edge normals.

## 10. What the Certificates Support

Each certificate proves $s(n) \le S$ for its own side, and therefore the verified values
the receipts derive, which are $S$ rounded up at the printed precision:

- $s(69) \le 8.82719465572975$, with `exact_form` `35308778622919/4000000000000`;
- $s(83) \le 9.63475764863195$, with `exact_form` `192695152972639/20000000000000`;
- $s(87) \le 9.83881526994915$, with `exact_form` `196776305398983/20000000000000`.

Each is above its certificate’s side, by $2.2$, $4.5$ and $5.1 \times 10^{-15}$, and
above the catalogue’s root by $1.1 \times 10^{-14}$, $8.7 \times 10^{-13}$ and
$8.9 \times 10^{-13}$. They do not establish $s(n)$ at most the printed decimal or the
root, that the catalogue’s packing at its own 30-digit precision is valid, that its side
is a root of the printed polynomial, the degree-672 polynomial, rigidity, or optimality.

**How a case’s verified upper lane should state it.** `verified_upper_bound` carries the
verified value and its `exact_form`, citing the replay evidence.
Under `bounds_agree_at_declared_precision` a verified value with an exact form agrees
with a reported decimal that has none within one unit of the last place; at 2, 87 and 89
units none of the three agrees, so the assurance check requires each case to keep a
blocker on its reported upper lane.
That blocker must be rewritten: “No formal certificate currently supports the tighter
reported upper bound” becomes false once the lane moves.
It should say that the verified value is the exact certificate’s side rounded up, how
far it trails the printed side, that the certificate comes from a binary64 parse whose
contacts overlap by rounding, and that the SVG’s own pose, or a pose refined on its
active contacts, would close most of it.
Unlike T-056’s three trailing counts, no `replay-failure` conflict is called for: there
the source’s own printed pose was measured above its printed side, and here nothing
contradicts the catalogue’s side.
The gap is the precision of the input this repository could obtain.
The section that calls the verified upper bound “a ceiling” at the grid is to be
rewritten for the new value.
None of the new values crosses a neighbour’s bound: the verified uppers at $n = 68, 82$
and $86$ are below them, and those at $70, 84$ and $88$ above.

## 11. Which Code Confirmed It

The catalogue names no checker for these packings, and no producer’s code ran here.
The deciding programs, `V-upper-bound-promotion` (whose deciding code is
`sqpack.witness.promote_rational` with `sqpack.verify`) and
`V-check-rational-witness-independent`, were written in this repository before these
results existed, the independent checker by 28 August 2026. Neither imports verification
code from outside the repository: the independent checker uses the standard library and
`sqpack.yamlio` only, and the promotion’s outside dependencies are PyYAML, jsonschema
and strif for loading and writing, and mpmath for choosing the rational angles.
Evan Daniel’s parse supplies the input and decides nothing.
The relation is therefore `independent-implementation`: the certified bounds are
confirmed, independently re-implemented.
That phrase applies to the certified values of §10 and never to the catalogue’s sides.
What the deciders’ authors read is not recorded beyond the independent checker’s module
statement, so this is not a clean-room record.
The catalogue’s own linked tools, among them Ellsworth’s `parse_svg_packing.py`, were
not used by either.
