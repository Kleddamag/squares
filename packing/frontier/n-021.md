# `s(21)` — open

Open. The best known packing gives `s(21) ≤ 5`. The verified lower bound is
`s(21) ≥ 5000/1001 = 4.995004995`, from Evan Daniel’s weighted certificate in
[`evand/square-packing`](../resources/web/evand-square-packing-2026-09-26/README.md),
which builds on Sam Burns’s and Gustavo Massaccesi’s weighted exact-rational covering
method; its `CREDITS.md` says the work was produced with an AI agent under human
direction. That leaves a gap of `5/1001 ≈ 0.004995`, the smallest of any open case in
this record. It raises this repository’s own `122/25 = 4.88` (`T-034`) by about `0.115`.

## The packing

The record catalogue does not picture `n = 21`: no arrangement has ever been found that
beats the trivial `⌈√21⌉ = 5` grid, so the grid is still the best known packing.
That is a statement about what has been searched, not a proof.

## The lower bound

The operative certificate is 4,604 rationally weighted points in `[0, 5000/1001]²`, 580
orbits of the square’s symmetry group, total weight `260057/12500 = 20.80456 < 21`, such
that every closed unit square in the container, at every angle, captures weight at least
`1`. The source built it by scaling a near-cover of `[0,5]²` down to side `5000/1001`
and re-optimising the weights with its exact verifier as the separation oracle.
Its Rust verifier decides the claim over a rational angle net at `N = 6000`, shrinking
each bin’s square by `1/(cos δ + sin δ)`; the least captured weight is `10000083/10⁷`.
That verifier was replayed here from the retained bytes at `N = 6000`, reproducing that
minimum at the same bin, `k = 684`, and the source’s exact Python re-check was run on a
sample of bins that includes it
([`E-n021-evand-5000-1001-source-replay`](evidence.yaml)); the source’s complete
re-check took 87 minutes on 12 workers and was not repeated.
A [review](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-09-27-evand-s32-s12.md) of 27
September found the bound sound as stated.
The certificate entered the source on 2026-09-23; this record first saw it on
2026-09-27.

The previous verified bound, from 2026-09-23, is this repository’s
[T-034 certificate](../cases/n21_fractional_certificate/certificate.json) at side
`122/25`: 1228 rationally weighted atoms on a `D4`-symmetric site set, total mass
`5036431/250000 = 20.145724 < 21`, and least covered mass `250001/250000`. An exact
event-cell sweep and an interval branch and bound independently decide its conditions
from the retained bytes and agree on that least mass, and a
[review](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-09-23-n21-122-25-certificate.md)
re-decided the same bytes by three routes and accepted them.
Only `Condition 2` mentions `n` among the five certificate conditions, so that atom set
certifies its side for every integer above its mass, which is 21 and upward.

Before it, the verified bound was `97/20 = 4.85`, from the
[T-021 certificate](../cases/n20_fractional_certificate/certificate.json): 1680 atoms of
total mass `19848723/1000000 = 19.848723`, which certifies `n = 20` and `n = 21` alike
and remains the verified `n = 20` bound.
The
[earlier T-020 certificate](../cases/n20_fractional_certificate/certificate-24-5.json)
at `24/5` remains valid and still supplies the verified `n = 19` bound.

wand125’s rectangle-density certificates reached `s(21) ≥ 997/200 = 4.985` on
2026-09-26, also above `T-034` and now superseded by the operative bound; they are
recorded with that source’s other bounds.
The DS7 survey’s Table 2 gives the decimal `4.7438` for `s(21)`, credited to Friedman,
without an exact identity or a rounding guarantee; it is kept as opaque reported
evidence. Nagamochi’s general closed form, which applies to every `N ≥ 4`, gives
`1 + √14`:

```
s(N) ≥ min{ ⌈√N⌉,  √(N − 2⌊√N⌋ + 1) + 1 }
```

## What the method can still add here

This repository’s generator fixes the shrunken side at `B = 9977/10000`, and a
certificate of that shape cannot exist above `⌈√n⌉·B = 4.9885`. The operative
certificate is above that line because its verifier shrinks each angle bin separately at
a much finer net, so the ceiling belongs to the generator’s settings, not to the
weighted-point method.
The source measures its own limit: the per-bin shrink costs about `2/N`, which must stay
below the margin `1 − t/5`, so each step toward `5` needs a finer net, and no finite net
reaches `5` itself.
