# `s(83)` — open

**External intake, 2026-10-02.** wand125’s
[linear-certificate source](../resources/web/wand125-linear-certificates-2026-10-02/README.md)
reports $s(83) \ge 187/20 = 9.35$ (T-073), from a measure of points, segments and
rectangles of total mass $8299999/100000 < 83$, accepted there at every net angle by its
linear verifier, a checker the 2 October review read with no blocking defect.
It is above Green’s reported $9.2667335\ldots$, which this case held by monotonicity, by
$0.083266\ldots$; the source’s “more than $0.0833$” is measured from a value below
Green’s. Its complete replay has not run here, so the verified lower bound is unchanged.
wand125’s README says parts of the work were produced with AI assistance under human
direction.

Open. The best known packing gives $s(83) \le 9.63482563$, and the strongest lower bound
independently verified here is $9.124038$ from Nagamochi’s general theorem, leaving a
gap of $0.5108$. General closed form: s(N) >= min(ceil(sqrt(N)), sqrt(N -
2*floor(sqrt(N)) + 1) + 1).

## The verified upper bound is a ceiling

`verified_upper_bound` for this case is $10$, which is **larger** than the best known
$9.63482562092335$ two fields above it.
It is not a tighter reading of the same packing and it is not the value of $s(83)$: it
is the strongest ceiling this repository can certify from its own evidence — the trivial
grid bound $\lceil\sqrt{83}\rceil = 10$ — and it trails the reported construction by
$0.36517437907665$.

Its `exact_form` is the exact form of that ceiling, never of $s(83)$. $s(83)$ is not
known exactly; `status` stays `open`, and the `mathematics` blocker in the frontmatter
names the gap. Read `reported_upper_bound` for the best known side length.

## The packing

Found by Károly Hajba in 2024, via extension of a smaller record.
Later improved by David W. Cantrell.
Its side length is algebraic of degree **24** over $\mathbb{Q}$.

## The lower bound

Before 2 October the selected external report was [Friedman DS7], which gives the
lower-bound expression `94*sqrt(2)/41 + 247/41` for $s(83)$ (approximately
$9.266733533246$). Friedman’s DS7 survey, Theorem 9, k=9, reports this bound at n=82;
reference [8] is Green’s private communication (2000). The source proof has not been
recovered.
The unavoidable-set argument DS7’s Figure 34 illustrates does not prove it: at
$k = 9$ that point pattern leaves a unit square empty
([review](../../docs/project/reviews/review-2026-10-02-green-ds7-theorem9.md)).
Inherited at n=83 by monotonicity.
That changed the reported source field only; the independently verified lower bound
remains $9.12403840464$. The [source audit](../devtools/audit_ds7_lower_bounds.py)
compares the exact theorem expressions separately from opaque table decimals.

The strongest lower bound independently verified in this record is Nagamochi’s general
closed form, which applies to every $N \ge 4$:

```
s(N) ≥ min{ ⌈√N⌉,  √(N − 2⌊√N⌋ + 1) + 1 }
```

See the [Frontier corpus summary](README.md#what-the-corpus-shows) for the current
aggregate count; source-reported bounds are recorded separately.
