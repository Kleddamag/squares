## 3. The Theorem and What Changed

**Theorem (R068).** Let `s(17)` be the least side of a closed square containing
seventeen closed unit squares with pairwise disjoint interiors, each at any orientation,
boundary contact allowed.
Then `s(17) > 116511/25000`. R067 is the same statement at `233009/50000`.

The reduction, the charging argument, the strict cores, the Möbius-expanded event sweep,
the boundary argument and the compactness step are those of §3 of the

not restate. What differs, certificate by certificate:

**Why the moved site changes no capacity.** A rule’s capacity is the largest number of
pairwise-disjoint winning subsets of its tuple, a property of the Boolean rule alone:
closed pairwise-disjoint cores capture pairwise-disjoint site subsets whatever the
sites’ positions, so at most that many cores can fire it.
Site positions enter the argument only through distinctness (two identities at one point
would still be captured together or not at all, and both checkers reject duplicates
anyway) and through containment in the container.
The moved site is `8.5×10⁻⁵` from its nearest neighbour and inside `[0, L]²`; the exact
capacity of all 86 patterns is 1 in R068 as in `4.66001`.

**The geometry contract, and the two steps the source asserts.** For a half-angle
interval `[a, b]` with representative `t` and core side `B`, with `c(u) = (1 − u²)/(1 +
u²)`, `s(u) = 2u/(1 + u²)`, the checkers verify at both endpoints `dot = c(t)c(u) +
s(t)s(u) > 0` and `|cross| = |c(t)s(u) − s(t)c(u)| ≤ dot`, then `h = max_{u∈{a,b}} (dot
+ |cross|)`, `A − B·h > 0`, `r = A·min_{u∈{a,b}} (c(u) + s(u))/2`, `B(c(t) + s(t))/2 ≤
  r < L/2`, and `0 < B < A`. Here `dot + |cross| = cos δ + |sin δ|` with `δ = θ(t) −
  θ(u)`, and the support of a square of side `B` at relative angle `δ` in a parent axis
direction is `B(cos δ + |sin δ|)/2`, so `A − B·h > 0` says the closed core lies strictly
inside the open parent at both endpoint angles.
`PROOF.md` asserts, as Kleddamag’s did, that the endpoint check “is valid throughout
each interval” and that `[r, L − r]²` “encloses all legal parent centres”.
  Both are true:

- *Endpoints suffice.* `δ(u)` is monotone in `u`, so over `[a, b]` it fills the interval
  between `δ(a)` and `δ(b)`; the endpoint checks put both in `[−π/4, π/4]`, hence all of
  it, and `|δ(u)| ≤ max(|δ(a)|, |δ(b)|)`. On `[0, π/4]` the function `cos δ + |sin δ| =
  √2 sin(|δ| + π/4)` is increasing in `|δ|`, so its maximum over the interval is `h`.
- *Envelope.* A parent at angle `θ` has legal centres
  `[A(cos θ + sin θ)/2, L − A(cos θ +
  sin θ)/2]²`, and `cos θ + sin θ = √2 sin(θ + π/4)` is concave on `[0, π/2]`, so its
  minimum over the interval is at an endpoint and `r ≤ A(c(u) + s(u))/2` for every `u ∈
  [a, b]`.

Both facts were also re-checked exactly at five interior points of every row of all
three certificates (`premises.py`), with the containment and envelope inequalities
holding at each; and every representative lies inside its interval, is its exact
midpoint, and satisfies `t² + 2t − 1 ≤ 0`, which the C++ sweep requires (`C ≥ S`). The
chain of each certificate is gapless from `0` to `207107/500000`, where `u² + 2u − 1 =
309449/250000000000 > 0`, so it passes `tan(π/8)` and covers `θ ∈ [0, π/4]`; a parent at
`θ ∈ (π/4, π/2)` is reflected alone across the container diagonal, where D4-invariance
of the charge (complete orbits, one weight each) gives its core the same charge.
The condition `B(c(t) + s(t))/2 ≤ r` is not needed for the theorem (it keeps a core
centred anywhere in the envelope inside the container, and extra centres can only lower
the minimum); it holds regardless.

The margins are thin and exact.
`A − B·h` lies in `[1.0000108×10⁻¹², 1.0002022×10⁻¹²]` for R067 and
`[1.0000007×10⁻¹², 1.0002021×10⁻¹²]` for R068, a hundredth of Kleddamag’s `10⁻¹⁰`; the
least R067 margin, `392478867597…/392474616038…×10⁻⁵⁴`, is the value `ACCEPTANCE.json`
reports (it falls on the same interval as `4.66001`’s least margin, row 2139 there, and
is exactly one hundredth of it: the same construction at a smaller target), and the
least R068 margin `952933981168…/952933310781…×10⁻⁵⁷` is the value the C++ ledger header
reports. `B/A` runs up to `0.9999993` at R068’s narrowest row.
Since every quantity is a rational compared exactly, the thinness costs nothing (F4).

**The counting.** Seventeen parents with disjoint interiors have pairwise disjoint
closed cores (each strictly inside its open parent), so a site lies in at most one core
and the total charge is at most `M`; every core, being the certified core of some row at
a legal centre, carries at least `Γ`; so `17Γ ≤ M`. The sweeps give `Γ = 1,000,026,844`
in both certificates, and `17Γ − M` is `54,340` for R067 and `7,404` for R068, so no
packing exists at `T`. The checkers assert only the certificate’s requested threshold,
`⌈M/17⌉` in both cases (`1,000,023,648` and `1,000,026,409`, surplus 8 and 9 over `M`),
and the launcher recomputes `17Γ − M > 0` from the row minima; R068’s `PROOF.md` says
explicitly that the requested threshold is not `Γ` (F3). Strictness: a packing at any
`S ≤ T` scales into the container at `T`, so none exists at any `S ≤ T`; centres and
orientations range over a compact set and containment and interior-disjointness are
closed conditions, so the least side is attained, and `s(17) > T`. That is the argument
`PROOF.md` gives, with monotonicity in the side left implicit.

## 5. Findings

Severity is blocking, non-blocking, or note.
**None is blocking.**

### F1. One method, two implementations, one of them new here; the native route still cannot load the certificate (blocks `C4`)

The Node checker is the reviewed one.
The C++ checker is Guzhou0806’s own and had not been read by this repository before; it
was read in full here.
Its front-end is a faithful re-expression of Kleddamag’s validation: arbitrary-precision
decimal parsing of every integer (Boost `property_tree` keeps values as strings, and a
`no_duplicates` pass rejects repeated JSON keys); the target identity
`L/A = normalized_target` with `L = 4613/1000` hard-coded; sites in the closed
container, distinct after D4 expansion into a sorted set, the same order the Node
checker uses; coefficients positive and sorted, threshold at most their sum, masks
distinct, nonzero, within `2ⁿ` and pairwise intersecting, `n ≤ 12`; orbit completeness
by the D4 image set of the first tuple; the budget `Σ w × images × cap` plus point
weights; a Möbius transform cached per pattern; the signed mass under `2⁵⁰` checked as
it accumulates; the interval chain with `a = cursor`, `0 < B < A`, the endpoint support
range, the margin, the envelope, and `cursor² + 2·cursor > 1` at the end; and a SHA-256
implementation self-tested on two vectors whose output on the 1.9 MB certificate equals
`sha256sum`’s. Two additions are real strengthenings: the capacity dynamic program
(`dp[mask] = max(1 + dp[mask ^ hit])` over winning `hit ⊆ mask`, requiring
`dp[2ⁿ − 1] ≤ cap`), and refusal to overwrite an existing output.
The sweep backend is the R052-4.62003 kernel unchanged: `cpp_int` with 512-bit inline
storage and no upper limit, an `int64` lazy segment tree under the same `2⁵⁰` mass
guard, `INF = 2⁶⁰`, Python-style floor division for the slab bottoms, and the same
`upper_bound(floor) − 1`, `lower_bound(ceil)` cell range as the Node checker, which is
exactly the set of open cells meeting the slab’s closed vertical extent.
It restricts the representative to `θ ≤ π/4` (`C ≥ S`), which every row satisfies.
The launcher `replay.js` partitions rows by `⌊N·i/w⌋`, requires every partition’s
status, range, digest, budget and threshold, compares minima and cell counts row by row,
requires each minimum at or above the requested threshold, recomputes the surplus, and
re-hashes the four inputs after the run.

So the certificate is decided by two implementations of one event-cell method, and this
review’s instrument is a third.
Under [`epistemics.md`](../../../epistemics.md) that is `C3`. The method-distinct route,
the native interval branch and bound behind BC-393, still holds only point and
`k`-of-`m` atoms (`packing/src/sqpack/fractional/parent_core.py` has no winning-subset
atom), so it cannot read these certificates; §7 sizes the change.

### F2. The two prose steps are still asserted, and no control ships for them (non-blocking)

Steps 5 and 6 of the trace are the `4.66001` review’s F2 again: `PROOF.md` asserts that
the endpoint checks make “the endpoint support bound valid throughout each interval” and
that `[r, L − r]²` “covers all legal parent centres”, and R067’s `non_strict_core`
control corrupts `B` to a value any endpoint test catches.
Both are true; §3 now gives the short proofs (monotonicity of `cos δ + |sin δ|` in `|δ|`
on `[0, π/4]`, concavity of `cos θ + sin θ`), and they were checked exactly at interior
points of every row.
A record citing either `PROOF.md` should cite §3 of this review alongside it.

### F3. `minimum_units` is the requested threshold, not `Γ` (note)

As at `4.66001` (its F3): the certificates carry `minimum_units = ⌈M/17⌉`,
`1,000,023,648` in R067 (Kleddamag’s value, unchanged, since `M` is unchanged) and
`1,000,026,409` in R068. The checkers assert it per row and the launcher recomputes `Γ`
and the surplus; R068’s `PROOF.md` states the distinction.
No record should quote either as `Γ`; `Γ` is `1,000,026,844` in both, the `4.66001`
value.

### F4. The containment margin was thinned a hundredfold, harmlessly (note)

Kleddamag chose `B` so that `A − B·max g ≈ 10⁻¹⁰`; both continuations use `≈ 10⁻¹²`, and
at R068’s narrowest row `B/A = 0.9999993`. In exact rational arithmetic a positive
margin of any size is a proof, and both checkers compare exact rationals, so this
changes nothing in the theorem.
It does mean that any future floating-point re-implementation of the geometry contract
would need about 13 significant digits merely to see the sign; the native route’s
interval arithmetic is exact and unaffected.

### F5. The ledger is flat, the minimum sits in the corner, and R068 has spent most of the surplus (note, and the planning fact)

Every row of R067 and of R068 has minimum `1,000,026,844`, the `4.66001` value, and at
every row sampled here the argmin is the corner cell `(r, r)`. R067 raised the side by
`0.00017` at fixed charge and lost nothing; the `4.640020` lemma addendum’s finding that
the fixed-weight charge falls in steps as the side grows did not bite yet.
R068 raised it by a further `0.00026` and had to pay: moving one site of the far-corner
two-of-three rule inward by `0.00008`, and adding `46,936` of budget in a four-site
point orbit, so that `17Γ − M` fell from `54,340` to `7,404`, a relative `4.4×10⁻⁷`, 435
units per core. The added charge is not load-bearing at any of the eleven rows sampled
here; it patches cells elsewhere in the catalogue.
Read with the flat ledger, the recipe “fixed charge, finer catalogue, rebuilt cores,
small patches” has about a seventh of its `4.66001` surplus left and is at its end; the
next step at this dictionary is a reweighting, which the `4.66001` review argued is
worth nothing on a ledger flat to `10⁻⁷`, or a new dictionary.
The source’s own next attempt is the latter (F8), and it failed at `4.6605`.

### F7. First nonzero point weights through this checker lineage (note)

Every certificate Kleddamag’s general-rule checkers have run had all `point_orbits`

### F8. The C010 local material is not a proof and does not claim to be; its counterexample is the useful part (note)
