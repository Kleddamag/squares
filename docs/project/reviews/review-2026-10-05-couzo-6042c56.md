## 2. Verdict

**No blocking defect.** For each count, the record’s verified upper bound $V$ is a true
upper bound on $s(n)$. Each committed certificate is a packing of $n$ exact unit squares
inside $[0, S]^2$, so $s(n) \le S \le V$. Each printed pose, translated, fits a square
of side $V$, decided with the true cosine and sine of each printed angle.
The claimed values, $s(208) \le 14.926534459703512$, $s(209) \le 14.953939011860643$,
$s(228) \le 15.604602454552252$, $s(263) \le 16.742270262031792$,

$s(306) \le 17.963438139777141$, are those $V$, and each is below the verified value

Nothing here bears on optimality, and no lower bound moves.

## 3. From Certificate to Claim

The argument is T-056’s, and the
[29 September review](review-2026-09-29-issue-227-upper-bound-packings.md#3-what-an-exact-corner-certificate-proves)
gives it in full. Re-derived for these seven, with each hypothesis the checkers assume:

1. **The certificate is a packing.** Each square’s four rational corners satisfy
   $e_0 \cdot e_0 = 1$, $e_0 \cdot e_1 = 0$, $e_2 = -e_0$ and $e_3 = -e_1$, so they are
   the vertices of a unit square.
   Every corner lies in $[0, S]^2$, and the container is convex, so every square lies
   inside it. For every pair, some edge direction of one of the two squares projects them
   to intervals that meet at most at an endpoint, so their interiors are disjoint.
   All three facts are decided over $\mathbb{Q}$ with no tolerance; contact is allowed
   and overlap is not.
2. **The bound is the certificate’s side, rounded up.** `S` is the certificate’s own
   extent. The verified value is $V = \max(\text{printed}, \lceil S \rceil_{15})$, the
   larger of the printed side and `S` rounded up at the printed fifteen decimals, so
   $S \le V$.
3. **The certificate is the author’s packing.** The promotion keeps every printed centre
   (18 significant digits, below the 36 it rounds to) and moves each angle by about
   $10^{-36}$ to make its cosine and sine rational.
   All seven were accepted at the first candidate, centre dilation 1. This hypothesis
   bears on attribution, not on the bound: a certificate that was not Couzo’s pose would
   still prove its own bound.
4. **The interval route bounds the printed pose itself.** Each vertex
   $c \pm e_1/2 \pm e_2/2$ is enclosed with the true $\cos\theta$ and $\sin\theta$ of
   the printed angle. The pose is translated so that its least $x$ and $y$ are zero,
   which leaves disjointness unchanged, and every vertex then lies in $[0, U]^2$ for
   $U = \max(\text{printed}, \lceil \text{extent}^{+} \rceil_{15})$. That gives
   $s(n) \le U$, and $U = V$ at every count.

The facts files carry the source’s representation: centre and angle, unit squares,
angles in radians, read from the header `x y theta(rad)`. Section 8 checks that
transcription against the upstream bytes.

## 6. The Review’s Own Computation

`third.py` was written for this review and is kept beside its log in the session’s
scratch space, not in the repository.
It reads the certificates and the facts as text with regular expressions, so it shares
no code with `sqpack`, `devtools` or any YAML library.

- **Exact part.** It reads each certificate into `Fraction` and checks the unit-square
  identities and containment in $[0, S]^2$, and whether the certificate touches its
  container. A broad phase on floating bounding boxes, widened by `1e-9`, leaves the
  remaining pairs, 606 to 936 per count.
  Each of those is decided by projecting all eight vertices onto the four edge
  directions over $\mathbb{Q}$. It then computes $V$ and the units above the printed
  side from the exact side.
- **Interval part.** It encloses each printed decimal in mpmath’s `iv` at 60 digits,
  after checking that the enclosure contains the exact decimal.
  It takes `iv.cos` and `iv.sin`, encloses all vertices, the extent and the frame
  clearance as placed, and compares them with the printed side exactly.
  Pairs whose printed centres lie at least $\sqrt{2}$ apart, tested exactly on the
  decimals, are pruned.
  Every other pair is decided by the vertex-projection test on the four edge directions.

## 7. The Case of `n = 306`

The certificate’s side is $17.96343813977714075\ldots$, `1.754e-15` above the printed
$17.963438139777139$, and the printed pose’s own extent is the same to 50 digits.
The printed side is therefore not the side of the printed pose in either arithmetic.
Rounded up at fifteen decimals, the extent gives $17.963438139777141$, 2 units of the
last place above the printed side.
That is more than the one unit `bounds_agree_at_declared_precision` accepts.

The record does what T-056 did at $n = 206$, 259 and 305, and the 29 September review
judged that handling correct.
`verified_upper_bound` carries $17.963438139777141$ and `reported_upper_bound` keeps the
printed side. The case record has a `replay-failure` conflict citing both replays, which
says that the interval route finds the gap in the pose and not in the rounding, and a
`mathematics` blocker naming the two ways to close it.
The verified value is still below T-056’s $17.963449907261179$ at that count, so the
ceiling moved down by `1.18e-5` even though it trails its report.
The tests now count four trailing Couzo counts, and the corpus count of ceilings that
trail their reports rose from 82 to 83, which is correct bookkeeping, not a regression.

## 9. The Tooling Changes

- **`upper_bound_packets.Source`** gains `layout`, `retrieved`, `control` and
  `supersedes`. The September packet keeps its retrieval date, its control (68, 31) and
  no `supersedes`, so its receipts and checks are unchanged; its `check` and its
  interval replay passed here.
  The new source names `control=(208, 2)` and
  `supersedes="franciscouzo-square-packing"`. `acquire_couzo` for a superseding packet
  keeps facts only where the printed side changed and still pins every upstream file.
  A pose replaced at an unchanged printed side would be skipped, which did not arise
  here: every changed file changed its side.
- **Chaining in `apply_upper_bound_packets`.** `plans()` keeps one plan per count, the
  latest registration’s, with the earlier plan as `previous`. `apply_case` applies the
  earlier plan first and then the later one.
  The paragraph on the replaced packing is replaced rather than appended, keyed on its
  opening words, and the evidence an earlier registration wrote, conflicts and blockers
  included, is treated as the chain’s own.
  Three tests of this ran here:
  - `--check` reports nothing, and a real run writes nothing, so the tool is idempotent
    on the committed records.
  - The seven case records were restored to their state before the import, T-056 alone,
    and the tool was run.
    The result equals the committed records, front matter included, apart from line
    wrapping, which the commit hook applies, and the verification-code table, which
    `devtools.render_case_verifiers` writes.
    The records were then restored to `HEAD`.
  - The new test `test_a_later_registration_takes_a_count_and_keeps_the_earlier_packing`
    holds the same facts and passes.
- **Supersession.** In `source-coverage.yaml` the seven `selected_overrides` move to the
  new source and cite T-092 and the side it replaces.
  Seven `superseded_reports` record T-056’s sides as superseded by the author’s own
  later side, and Casson’s entries at six counts now name the new source.
  The new coverage entry is `current-report` with the three evidence entries.
- **The regularizer’s certificate.** With two certificates at a count, the atlas index
  now names the one whose frame matches.
  At all seven counts it names the 3 October certificate with
  `matches_retained_certificate: true`, and $n = 206$, 207 and 305 still name the
  September one.
- **The independence record.** `V-upper-bound-intervals` now names its module as its
  `independence_record`, and the two interval entries of 30 September leave the
  grandfathered list, which only shrinks.
  Finding 5 qualifies what that record says.
