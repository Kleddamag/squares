# `s(104)` — open

**External intake, 2026-10-02.** wand125’s
[linear-certificate source](../resources/web/wand125-linear-certificates-2026-10-02/README.md)
reports $s(101) \ge 257/25 = 10.28$ (T-073), from a measure of points, segments and
rectangles accepted there at every net angle by its linear verifier, a checker the 2
October review read with no blocking defect.
A packing of 104 squares contains one of 101, so the bound holds here by monotonicity,
above Green’s reported $10.2467362\ldots$, which this case held by monotonicity.
Its complete replay has not run here, so the verified lower bound is unchanged.

Open. The best known packing gives $s(104) \le 10.70710679$, and the strongest lower
bound independently verified here is $10.219544$ from Nagamochi’s general theorem,
leaving a gap of $0.4876$. General closed form: s(N) >= min(ceil(sqrt(N)), sqrt(N -
2*floor(sqrt(N)) + 1) + 1).

## The verified upper bound is a ceiling

`verified_upper_bound` for this case is $11$, which is **larger** than the best known
$10.70710678118654$ two fields above it.
It is not a tighter reading of the same packing and it is not the value of $s(104)$: it
is the strongest ceiling this repository can certify from its own evidence — the trivial
grid bound $\lceil\sqrt{104}\rceil = 11$ — and it trails the reported construction by
$0.29289321881346$.

Its `exact_form` is the exact form of that ceiling, never of $s(104)$. $s(104)$ is not
known exactly; `status` stays `open`, and the `mathematics` blocker in the frontmatter
names the gap. Read `reported_upper_bound` for the best known side length.

## The packing

Found by an unrecorded author, via a diagonal-strip construction.

## The lower bound

The strongest lower bound independently verified in this record is Nagamochi’s general
closed form, which applies to every $N \ge 4$:

```
s(N) ≥ min{ ⌈√N⌉,  √(N − 2⌊√N⌋ + 1) + 1 }
```

Source-reported bounds are recorded separately from this independently verified theorem.

Before 2 October the reported lower-bound expression for $s(104)$ was
`18*sqrt(5)/101 + 2*sqrt(2) + 709/101` (approximately $10.24673626925$). Friedman’s DS7
survey, Theorem 9, k=10, reports this bound at n=101; reference [8] is Green’s private
communication (2000). The source proof has not been recovered.
Inherited at n=104 by monotonicity.
It was a source-reported lower bound; it did not replace the verified bound above.
The exact specialization and comparison are retained by
[`audit_ds7_lower_bounds`](../devtools/audit_ds7_lower_bounds.py).
