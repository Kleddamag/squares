# `s(305)` — open

Open. The best known published packing, Francisco Couzo’s (T-056), gives
$s(305) \le 17.952959459023539$, and the strongest lower bound independently verified
here is $17.492422$ from Nagamochi’s general theorem, leaving a gap of $0.4605$. General
closed form: s(N) >= min(ceil(sqrt(N)), sqrt(N - 2*floor(sqrt(N)) + 1) + 1).

## The verified upper bound is a ceiling

`verified_upper_bound` for this case is $17.952959459023541$, proved by the exact
rational certificate of Francisco Couzo’s packing
(`E-franciscouzo-2026-09-27-exact-replay`). It is **larger** than the best known
$17.952959459023539$ two fields above it, by `2e-15`.

It is not the value of $s(305)$ and not a different packing: it is the certificate’s own
side, $17.95295945902354086517\ldots$, rounded up at the fifteen decimals the source
prints. The source writes each coordinate as a binary64 value, and rounded to rationals
those poses close only at a side 2 units of the last place above the printed one, so the
printed side itself is not certified here.
The `replay-failure` conflict and the `mathematics` blocker in the frontmatter record
the difference. Read `reported_upper_bound` for the best known side length.

## The packing

Francisco Couzo’s
[`square-packing`](../resources/web/franciscouzo-square-packing-2026-09-27/README.md)
reports a packing of side $17.952959459023539$ for this count, dated 27 September 2026
and unchanged at the pinned revision `f3c5a52` of 27 September 2026 (T-056). Its first
packing for this count, of side $17.953672188874847$, is dated 24 September 2026 at
10:28 UTC by the author’s clock.
The repository names no method and no tolerance, and itself states no AI assistance; its
author said on [issue #227](https://github.com/jlevy/squares/issues/227) that he found
the 102 and 103 packings “with the help of Claude”.

This repository certifies it exactly.
The retained decimal pose rounds to an exact rational packing at centre dilation 1, of
side $17.9529594590235408652\ldots$, `1.865e-15` above the printed side, and every pair
and every wall is decided over $\mathbb{Q}$ twice, by the promotion’s exact
separating-axis test and by an independent checker that shares no code with it
([receipt](../resources/web/franciscouzo-square-packing-2026-09-27/README.md#certified-here)).
That proves $s(305) \le 17.952959459023541$, the verified upper bound; it says nothing
about optimality. Interval arithmetic on the printed pose itself, with each angle’s true
cosine and sine and no rational rounding, decides every pair and wall again and gives
the same verified upper bound
([interval route](../resources/web/franciscouzo-square-packing-2026-09-27/README.md#interval-route)).

Griffin Casson’s
[`square-packing`](../resources/web/casson-square-packing-2026-09-23/README.md),
committed on 23 September 2026 at 22:45 UTC−6 (04:45 UTC on the 24th) with the help of
Claude as its README says, reports a packing of side $17.96053668235995815$ for this
count, larger than Couzo’s by $0.00757722333641915$. By the authors’ clocks Casson’s is
the earlier: Couzo’s first packing for this count, of side $17.953672188874847$, is
dated 24 September 2026 at 10:28 UTC. The record states both dates and infers nothing
about whether either packing derives from the other.

## The lower bound

The strongest lower bound independently verified in this record is Nagamochi’s general
closed form, which applies to every $N \ge 4$:

```
s(N) ≥ min{ ⌈√N⌉,  √(N − 2⌊√N⌋ + 1) + 1 }
```

Source-reported bounds are recorded separately from this independently verified theorem.
