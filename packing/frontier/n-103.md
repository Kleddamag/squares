# `s(103)` — open

Open. The best known published packing, Francisco Couzo’s (T-056), gives
`s(103) ≤ 10.703516755580015`, and the strongest lower bound independently verified here
is `10.165151` from Nagamochi’s general theorem, leaving a gap of `0.5384`. General
closed form: s(N) >= min(ceil(sqrt(N)), sqrt(N - 2*floor(sqrt(N)) + 1) + 1).

## The packing

Francisco Couzo’s
[`square-packing`](../resources/web/franciscouzo-square-packing-2026-09-27/README.md)
reports a packing of side `10.703516755580015` for this count, dated 26 September 2026
and unchanged at the pinned revision `f3c5a52` of 27 September 2026 (T-056). Its first
packing for this count, of side `10.703583456926136`, is dated 23 September 2026 at
01:35 UTC by the author’s clock and was committed again on 24 September 2026 at 10:33
UTC. The repository names no method and no tolerance, and itself states no AI
assistance; its author said on [issue #227](https://github.com/jlevy/squares/issues/227)
that he found the 102 and 103 packings “with the help of Claude”.

This repository certifies it exactly.
The retained decimal pose rounds to an exact rational packing at centre dilation 1, of
side `10.7035167555800151510…`, `1.510e-16` above the printed side, and every pair and
every wall is decided over `ℚ` twice, by the promotion’s exact separating-axis test and
by an independent checker that shares no code with it
([receipt](../resources/web/franciscouzo-square-packing-2026-09-27/README.md#certified-here)).
That proves `s(103) ≤ 10.703516755580016`, the verified upper bound; it says nothing
about optimality.

Griffin Casson’s
[`square-packing`](../resources/web/casson-square-packing-2026-09-23/README.md),

at 10:33 UTC. [Issue #227](https://github.com/jlevy/squares/issues/227), opened on 23

repository’s clocks; the issue gives no side.
The record states both dates and infers nothing about whether either packing derives
from the other. Its README says the work was done by Griffin Casson “with the help of
Claude (Anthropic)”.

### The previous best known packing

Before this intake the best known packing was the UnitSquare Project’s, of side
`10.703790283762427`, below the Kingbird catalogue’s `10.70383477210707`.

The UnitSquare Project’s 29 July 2026 release improves the public Schadt-Ellsworth
parent by `0.000044488344643`. The release classifies the result as a construction-only
upper bound and says it used outward-rounded interval arithmetic, a 300-digit
zero-tolerance recomputation, and an independent published checker.
The public release does not include the interval boxes, governed receipt, or replayable
checker needed to inspect the formal claim.
This repository recorded the value as reported.
The formal lane held the exact `11 × 11` grid construction until this intake.

## The lower bound

The strongest lower bound independently verified in this record is Nagamochi’s general
closed form, which applies to every `N ≥ 4`:

```
s(N) ≥ min{ ⌈√N⌉,  √(N − 2⌊√N⌋ + 1) + 1 }
```

Source-reported bounds are recorded separately from this independently verified theorem.

The reported lower-bound expression for `s(103)` is
`18*sqrt(5)/101 + 2*sqrt(2) + 709/101` (approximately `10.24673626925`). Friedman’s DS7
survey, Theorem 9, k=10, reports this bound at n=101; reference [8] is Green’s private
communication (2000). The source proof has not been recovered.
Inherited at n=103 by monotonicity.
This is a source-reported lower bound; it does not replace the verified bound above.
The exact specialization and comparison are retained by
[`audit_ds7_lower_bounds`](../devtools/audit_ds7_lower_bounds.py).
